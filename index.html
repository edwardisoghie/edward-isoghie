§ 01

About

I'm a PhD candidate in Industrial & Systems Engineering and a human factors researcher at the University of Louisville's Center for Human Systems Engineering. I study how people perform inside complex, safety-critical work systems1 — where attention, workload, and design decisions determine whether things go right or wrong.

My work runs on mixed methods: task analysis, structured interviews, and observation on one side; usability testing and statistical analysis on the other.2 The aim is always the same — turn what I learn about human performance into design and training that actually help people.

Before Louisville I worked across consulting and safety-critical operations — from digital transformation at Deloitte to distribution operations at an electricity utility — where I first saw how much human factors decides real-world reliability.

1. Safety-critical: systems where a single use error carries real consequence — medical devices, manufacturing, power distribution.
2. Mixed methods is a throughline of my research, including how engineers themselves are taught to use them.
